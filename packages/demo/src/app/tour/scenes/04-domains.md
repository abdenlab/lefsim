Pairs of CTCF sites facing each other can lead to frequent and lasting loops. More generally, a landscape of CTCF sites tends to produce "domains" that partially fence off the flux of loops over shorter regions of chromatin.

Domains like these, a few hundred thousand base pairs long, are seen across the genome. They're called topologically associating domains, or TADs. They are an emergent property of loop extrusion with barriers.

The occupancy view averages where the legs sit over time. Extruder legs pile up at domain boundaries (CTCF sites).
