[Cohesin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohesin) and [condensin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensin) are ring-shaped protein complexes that fold chromosomes by
**extruding loops**. Here, a single *loop-extruding factor* (LEF) sits on a
stretch of chromatin, drawn as a line of 3,000 sites.

It binds at one site, and its two *legs* (anchor points on chromatin) walk outward, one step at a time,
gathering the fiber between them into a growing loop, consuming energy (ATP) as it goes. This is depicted by the arc on the stage. The
kymograph below traces each leg's position over time. The newest positions appear at the top.

After a while the LEF spontaneously lets go. To simulate a steady state LEF density, it binds again somewhere else to start a new loop. In reality, there are nuclear factors and complexes that facilitate loading and unloading.
