Cohesin and condensin are ring-shaped protein complexes that fold chromosomes by
**extruding loops**. Here, a single *loop-extruding factor* (LEF) sits on a
stretch of chromatin, drawn as a line of 3,000 sites.

It binds at one site, and its two legs walk outward, one step at a time,
gathering the fiber between them into a growing loop: the arc on the stage. The
kymograph below traces each leg's position over time, newest at the top.

After a while the LEF lets go, and it binds again somewhere else to start a new
loop.
