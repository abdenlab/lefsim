Pairs of CTCFs facing each other fence off a stretch of chromatin. Loops grow
until both legs are held at its edges, so the stretch folds largely on itself: a
**domain**.

The occupancy view averages where the legs sit over time. Extruders pile up at
the CTCFs, which stand out as peaks at every boundary.

Domains like these, a few hundred thousand base pairs long, are seen across the
genome. They're called topologically associating domains, or TADs.
